Brand essence
Before any visual decision

What the material has to feel like

An identity is not a look that gets picked — it is the consequence of a commercial position. Deal Pulse stands in a market crowded with coupon sites and needs to read as a platform a large advertiser deals with, not a codes website. Every decision in this manual serves that difference.

Position

The verified deal, not the longest list

We do not compete on catalogue length. We compete on what is published being true — so the visual must say "precision", not "abundance".

Personality

Calm · precise · confident

No discount shouting, no attention-chasing red. Trust is built with white space and discipline, not with density of elements.

Voice

A short sentence that says something

Contemporary standard Arabic without affectation. No exaggeration and no absolute promises — every line defensible to a legal team.

What we avoid

The market's stock symbols

No palm tree, no desert, no ornamental Arabic pasted on, no national green as a default. Locality comes from language and content.

The governing test

Would this material survive being sent to a marketing lead at a listed company?

If the answer is no, the fault is usually one of three: element density without hierarchy, a loud colour outside the palette, or copy promising what cannot be measured. Fix them in that order.

The mark
Anatomy

Three elements, each with a job

The ticket

A closed container with two side notches and a dotted perforation. It says "coupon" without writing the word — and it is what makes the brand recognisable without the name.

The DP monogram

The two letters inside the ticket — the only element that survives small sizes. Icon, avatar and favicon all derive from it.

The pulse line

The green wave crossing the monogram. It carries the "pulse" in the name and is the mark's only colour — nothing else is coloured.

Sizes & clear space
The breaking point — measured, not estimated

Where the full mark stops
and the monogram starts

Binding rules

  • 32mm (about 120px) minimum width for the full mark with the name
  • Below 17mm (about 64px) the name drops — use the ticket alone
  • Below 9mm (about 32px) use DP alone, no ticket frame
  • Favicon, avatar and app icon: always from DP

Why these numbers

  • The ticket frame eats the height, leaving DP at 40% of it at 32px
  • The Arabic name becomes a grey smudge below 64px — measured on this asset
  • Padding baked into icon files shrinks the mark a further 45%
  • Numbers measured on the files, not judged by eye
Clear space

No element — text, image or edge — comes closer to the mark than the height of the "D" in the monogram on any side. The dashed frame on the previous page shows it. On busy grounds or photography: use the knockout version on a clear area, never the mark directly over an image.

Misuse
What breaks the mark

Eight errors that void consistency

These are not preferences. Each one breaks either recognition, legibility, or consistency across surfaces.

Never do this to the mark

  • Recolour it, or colour the ticket
  • Stretch or squash it off its 1.662:1 ratio
  • Add a shadow, gradient or outer stroke
  • Place it over a busy image with no clear area
  • Set the name in another typeface beside it
  • Rotate or tilt it
  • Use the full mark as an app icon
  • Put it next to the name set as text (duplication)

The correct alternative

  • The ready light or knockout version
  • Set one dimension; the other follows
  • Clear space separating it from content
  • A solid colour plate behind it if needed
  • The name from the mark itself
  • Horizontal alignment, always
  • The DP monogram for icons
  • Icon only where the name is set as text
The header rule

In headers: icon plus the name as text — never the full mark

The header already sets the name as text, so placing the full mark beside it prints the name twice. The rule: where the name is written, use the icon; where it is not, use the full mark.

Contrast
What separates a system from a colour palette

Every pair measured — the floor is 4.5:1

Values computed with the WCAG 2.1 formula on the palette itself. Small text needs 4.5:1; large text (18pt and up, or 14pt bold and up) and interface elements need 3:1.

The derived rule

The light green is seen, not read

#10B981 is the brand colour, but it fails as text on white (2.54:1). Hence one ramp with three jobs: g-500 for the mark and accents · g-600 for graphics and arrows · g-700 for any surface carrying text. Breaking this produces material that looks right on your screen and fails in print and on a weaker display.

Typography
One family, two scripts

IBM Plex Sans Arabic —
chosen for function, not taste

Why this one specifically

Because its Arabic shaping is complete. Popular faces (Almarai, Tajawal, Dubai) are missing presentation forms such as U+FE95 and U+FE8D, so any system that reshapes Arabic with them prints empty boxes — a fault that fails silently, with no error.

And why it fits the personality

Geometric and disciplined without being cold, with a Latin sibling on the same skeleton — so Arabic and English in one piece look like family, not neighbours. Its licence is open (OFL), so nothing blocks scale.

Applications — which version goes where

Profile & proposals

Full mark on the cover at about 92mm wide, then the name as text in each page header. The mark is not repeated inside the body.

Website

Header: DP monogram plus the name as text. Favicon: DP alone, square, a multiple of 48. Full mark in the footer and on auth pages.

Telegram & mini app

Bot avatar: DP on ink. Inside the interface: a tightly cropped mark with the padding applied in CSS, not baked into the file.

Social

Avatar: DP. Posts: a small full mark in a fixed corner, or no mark at all relying on colour and type — consistency comes from those.

The rule that crosses everything: consistency does not come from repeating the logo, it comes from repeating colour, type and spacing. A piece with no logo but our palette and typeface is recognised; a piece with the logo and foreign colours is not.

Delivery checklist
Before shipping any material

Twelve questions — one failure stops the ship

The mark

  • Is it 32mm or wider if the full mark is used?
  • Is clear space preserved on every side?
  • Is the icon DP rather than the full mark?

Colour

  • Is every colour from the closed palette?
  • Is green under 15% of the area?
  • Does every text-on-green surface use g-700?

Type

  • Is IBM Plex the only family?
  • Are sizes from the scale, not improvised?
  • Is the minority script wrapped?

Delivery

  • Are the fonts embedded in the PDF?
  • Zero glyphs outside IBM Plex and DejaVu?
  • Is nothing clipped at the page edge?
One last test

Is the material recognisable without reading the name?

Cover the logo with your thumb. If the piece still reads as Deal Pulse — through colour, type and discipline — the identity is working. If it becomes anonymous, the logo was carrying the brand alone, and that is a fragile system.
